# Exploded view

Read this when the learner's gap is about structure: what something is made of, in which order, and why each part is there. The object is pulled apart along the axis it was assembled on, so the order of the parts is the explanation. Every part has a job; a part without one is cut.

This is a Workbench mode. Everything in [workbench.md](workbench.md) applies: the learning boundary, glass, motion, spacing and delivery. This file adds only what is specific to exploded views. The kit in [exploded-view-kit.js](../assets/exploded-view-kit.js) implements the camera, the renderer, picking and the part popover; copy it inline into the output so the file stays standalone. Start a new exploded view from [keyboard-switch.html](../examples/keyboard-switch.html): keep its `<head>` styles and the inlined kit unchanged, and replace only the title, intro and the parts script at the end. [keyboard-switch.html](../examples/keyboard-switch.html) (vertical stack) and [battery-cell.html](../examples/battery-cell.html) (horizontal, along the cell's axis) are the reference examples. The docked toolbar, popover, spacing and motion follow every Workbench guardrail; this mode has no exceptions. The keyboard legend sits in the header area in the upper third, not under the toolbar.

## Plan it

1. **Truth table first.** Before any geometry, write the parts as data: name, one-sentence job, real dimensions and the one or two numbers a learner would ask about (travel, force, thickness, capacity). Use typical values from a datasheet or standard and say "typical" where they vary. The geometry, the popover and the readout all read this table.
2. **Pick the assembly axis.** Parts move apart the way they were put together: `vertical` for stacks (a key switch, a phone, a PCB), `horizontal` for things assembled side by side (a battery pack, a drivetrain, a book binding), `radial` for things built around a centre (a motor, layers of a cable), or a per-part vector when the real disassembly is mixed. The axis is a choice about meaning, not about fitting the frame.
3. **Explode far enough to see into every part.** In the exploded state the clear gap between two neighbours along the axis is 0.6 × the larger of their footprints (the width across the axis), so from any elevation on the L-curve down to about 30° you can see the top of the lower part and the underside of the upper one (a stem's cross, a socket, a spring seat). Compute offsets cumulatively from each part's real extent, not from a fixed share of the total height.
4. **5 to 12 parts.** Fewer and nothing is learned; more and the learner stops reading. Merge screws and clips into the part they hold unless the fastener is the lesson.
5. **Order is reading order.** Parts are listed in assembly order. Up and Down in the popover walk that order.

## Draw it

- Build parts from the kit's primitives (box, rounded box, cylinder, frustum, ring, helix, extruded convex polygon) placed in millimetres, in real proportions. Small true details (a spring's turns, a stem's cross, pins) carry more than surface decoration.
- Parts read as drawn objects: flat-ish shading plus thin crease lines on every edge sharper than 30°, so a grey box never dissolves into a grey blob.
- The object always fills the view and is never cut off: fit the camera to the assembly's swept envelope (its projected bounds sampled over a full turn at the current elevation and explode amount), so it fills about 60 % of the free height in the overview, sits centred in the free space by its screen bounds rather than its 3D centre, and does not breathe while it spins. The L-curve scales that fitted distance instead of using fixed units.
- No coplanar faces and no z-fighting: holes, discs and decals are real geometry offset at least 0.05 mm from the surface they sit on, drawn with polygon offset, and the near/far ratio stays at or below 1 : 1000. Check by scrolling slowly through the whole L-curve with the part closest to a surface open.
- Every part keeps its contrast in both themes: its lightness differs from the ground by at least 0.15 (OKLab L). The kit maps the material tiers per theme (dark theme L' = 0.38 + (L − 0.30) · 0.93, light theme L' = 0.22 + (L − 0.30) · 0.97), so a dark keycap is mid-dark grey on the dark ground and a clear sleeve is mid-light grey on the light ground.
- Long horizontal rows (length more than twice their other extents) start with the row near screen-horizontal (default yaw −15°, compact objects −35°) and are fitted to their bounds at that default yaw, so they use the screen's width; while turning, their ends may pass under the docked bars. Compact objects are fitted to their yaw-swept envelope so their size does not change while turning.
- The object stands on a visible floor: always a true circle, never an ellipse or a shape stretched to the object's footprint. It is centred on the turn axis under the lowest part, with radius r = max(0.6 × the largest dimension of the exploded assembly's bounding box on any axis, 1.15 × the largest horizontal distance of any part from the axis). So a tall stack gets a floor wide enough to read from above, and a long row's ends stay on the floor in every turn. The floor's size never changes while turning or tilting; 0.07 OKLab L off the ground (lighter in dark, darker in light), solid to 0.7 of its radius and fading to transparent at the rim, with the contact shadow under the lowest part. Check it in a screenshot of both themes: the floor centre must differ from the ground by at least 0.05 L. An object floating in the dot field with no floor is a defect.
- Spatial order equals list order. Along the explode direction, part n+1 sits next to part n, so Up and Down always move the camera to a neighbour, never across the whole object. Choose the order so parts of similar shape are not adjacent where an alternative reading order is equally true.
- Materials are neutral lightness tiers, not colours: dark plastic, light plastic, metal, board. The open part takes the Ember gradient (amber at its top, coral at its base); every other part stays neutral. No text inside the 3D scene.
- The stage is the Workbench field (ember floor, contrast-locked dots). The canvas is transparent over it.

## Interaction (the kit implements these; keep them)

- **The L-curve.** Scroll or pinch moves the camera along one L-shaped path: a vertical leg that descends from a bird's-eye view (elevation about 69°) to eye level (about 5°), a rounded corner, then a horizontal leg that moves in at eye level. Scrolling out runs the path back up. Each leg gets a fixed share of the scroll (descent 45 %, corner 15 %, approach 40 %) and is uniform by arc length inside it, so the close-up is not squeezed into the last few ticks.
- **Turn and tilt.** Left-drag sideways turns the object around its own vertical axis, with a little inertia; it never bobs up or down while turning, because the view is centred on a turn-invariant point. Dragging up and down tilts the view (elevation offset, clamped to 2°–85°) on top of the L-curve. Arrow keys left and right turn; up and down tilt while no part is open. Reset view clears the tilt.
- **Idle spin.** In the upper part of the path, after a few seconds without input and with no part open, the object turns slowly on its own axis. Any input stops it. Reduced motion disables it.
- **Parts.** Hover lifts a part's lightness. Click opens its glass popover beside it: name, position (3 / 8), the job sentence, its numbers. The popover goes to the side of the open part that is free of the other parts: left or right of a vertical stack, above or below a horizontal row. It never covers another part of the object; check this with the projected bounds of all parts, not only the open one. Up and Down walk to the neighbouring part; Close, Esc or a click on empty space returns to the whole. The camera target eases to the open part, and it eases again when Up, Down or a click switches straight from one open part to another.
- **Assembled and exploded.** A segmented control in the docked toolbar (and the key E) moves the parts together or apart. Views open exploded.
- **Keyboard and screen readers.** Every action has a key and a button. The kit keeps a hidden ordered list of parts as buttons and announces the open part in a live region.

## Learning boundary

In a hint artifact, only parts the learner has already established carry their job and numbers. The others show as "Part 4" with no description in the HTML, the data or the accessible names. Opening a part does not unlock the next hint.

## Inspect

Look at the file in a browser: bird's-eye, the corner, eye level close up, one open popover, light and dark, reduced motion, a narrow viewport. Repeat the framing and popover checks at landscape (1280 × 720, 1920 × 1080), near-square (870 × 900) and portrait (390 × 844) windows, and walk every part with Up and Down down to the lowest one: each open part must sit fully inside the free area with its popover beside it. Check that the parts' order and numbers match the truth table.
